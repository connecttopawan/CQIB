Chapter 1

What is CASB?

Think of CASB as a very old but important filing cabinet inside the bank — still working after decades, full of account records.

🔤 Full Name

CASB = Current Account & Savings Bank
It's the banking system used to store and manage customer account information for both individuals and businesses.

Why is it called "Legacy"?

A legacy system is one that's old but still in use. CASB was built in the mainframe era — long before cloud, APIs, or real-time systems existed.

🖥️
Mainframe-based

Runs on big iron computers that process batches of jobs overnight, not on-demand.

📁
File-based

Data comes as flat files, not through APIs. Think of CSV/text dumps every night.

⏰
T+1 Data

Yesterday's data arrives today. Real-time updates? Not possible here.

📜
Decades Old

This system has been running since the 1990s. Historic, reliable — and stubborn.

💡 Savings Accounts — A Note

The "SB" (Savings Bank) part in CASB is largely gone. The bank no longer offers savings account products. So today, CASB is mainly about Current Accounts.

Why Does CASB Still Matter?
1
Massive Historical Data Contains account records stretching back decades. Irreplaceable for regulatory and audit needs.
2
Many Active Accounts Thousands of business and individual current accounts are still managed through CASB.
3
Downstream Dependencies Reporting platforms, analytics tools, and compliance systems all depend on CASB data.
4
Source of Truth For current accounts, CASB is still the official source. Other systems get their data from here.
Chapter 2

Types of Accounts in CASB

CASB doesn't just hold one kind of account. Click each type below to learn what it's for.

💼 Current Accounts (CA) ▼
The most common account type in CASB today. Used by both individuals and businesses for:

✅ Daily deposits and withdrawals
✅ Business transactions
✅ Overdraft facilities (spending more than what's in the account)

These are not interest-bearing — they're purely transactional.
💰 Savings Accounts (SB) — Legacy ▼
These were personal accounts that earned interest. The bank no longer offers them, so you won't see new ones.

However, old historical savings account data still lives in CASB, which is why the "SB" in the name remains.
🏢 Entity Accounts ▼
Accounts held by businesses, corporations, or other organisations (not individuals).

Examples: company operating accounts, trust accounts, government accounts.
👔 Staff Accounts ▼
Accounts held by bank employees. They may have special terms like fee waivers or preferential rates.

CASB tracks these with a special staff flag so they can be treated differently in reporting.
🏛️ Office / Operational Accounts ▼
Internal accounts used by the bank itself — for example, fee collection accounts, error correction suspense accounts, or nostro accounts.

These are admin/housekeeping accounts, not customer-facing.
🔗 Loan-related / Overdraft Accounts ▼
CASB also stores information for overdraft-linked loan accounts. If a customer goes into overdraft, CASB can hold the linked credit limit and balance information.
✅ Key Takeaway

CASB holds many account types — but the most important one today is the Current Account. When you query CASB data, most records you'll see will be CAs.

Chapter 3

Old vs New: Product Setup

How a bank "defines" a product has changed completely. Let's walk through the old way, the new way, and why both still exist.

The Old Way — Genasi & GDW

Every banking product (like "Business Current Account - Corporate") had to be defined somewhere — what features it has, what rules apply. The old tool for this was called Genasi Rules.

ToolWhat It DidStatus
Genasi RulesOld rule-based product configuration in GDW🔴 Being replaced
DDM (Difference Data Manager)Managed metadata and registered product definitions🔴 Replaced by DRM
DRM (Data Reference Manager)Modern central metadata store for product reference data🟢 Active
GDW (Global Data Warehouse)Central enterprise data store; receives cleaned CASB data🟢 Active
Variable (V) vs Fixed (F) Components

CASB describes every product using components. Each component is either:

🔀
Variable (V)

Can change per customer or over time. Examples: variable interest rate, changing overdraft limit, dynamic fees.

🔒
Fixed (F)

Always the same for all customers on this product. Examples: fixed service fee, fixed account type, fixed interest rate.

The New Way — NAP Product Master
🆕 What is NAP Product Master?

It's the bank's modern central product catalog — a single master list of all products.

Every product gets a unique PCID (Product Catalog ID). This PCID is then used across all modern platforms and digital apps to refer to that product consistently.

Think of PCID like a product's serial number. Instead of different systems knowing the same product by different names, every system now uses the same PCID.

⚠️ The Problem

CASB is a mainframe system. It can't connect to NAP directly — it has no APIs, no real-time connections. So how do old CASB products get a modern PCID?

Chapter 4

The BRE Bridge 🌉

CASB is too old to talk to the new product system. So a smart "translator" was built between them.

🔧 BRE = Product Master Business Rule Engine

BRE is an application that acts as a translation layer. It takes old CASB product data and maps it to the modern PCID system.

1
CASB sends NDH files (old product files) These are legacy flat files containing product codes in old format that CASB understands.
2
BRE receives the NDH files The Business Rule Engine reads the old product codes and applies its mapping rules.
3
BRE matches each product to its PCID Like a translator, BRE knows that "CASB product code XYZ" = "PCID 45678" in the modern catalog.
4
Output: Standardised product mappings Now every old CASB product has a modern PCID it can use in downstream systems.
5
ADA stores BOTH mappings The old Genasi-based mapping AND the new PCID-based mapping are kept side by side in ADA.
💡 Why Two Mappings?

Because some systems still use the old Genasi-based product codes, and some use the new PCID. ADA keeps both so every downstream consumer can get what they need.

For a single account, you may see two product relationships — that's normal and expected!

Which mapping to use?

It depends on your business use case. Ask yourself:

  • Am I building a new application or report? → Use the 🆕 PCID mapping
  • Am I working with old GDW-based logic or legacy reports? → Use the 📜 Genasi mapping

Chapter 6

Working with ADA 🛠️

ADA is where you actually query CASB data. Here are the golden rules and handy tips every analyst needs to know.

How to Find CASB Metadata
📍 Navigation Path

go/ADA → Kangaroo Dashboard → BA0176
BA0176 is CASB's application ID in ADA. Start here to explore its metadata, table inventory, and data dictionary.

Silver Layer Tables in ADA

CASB Silver includes these subject areas (via go/silverbysubject):

Arrangements Organisation Units Products Interest Components Limit Tables Fee Overrides Customer-AR Relationships Tax Tables AR Relationship Tables Payments
🔑 Golden Rule: Always Join AR Translation
⚠️ Critical Query Tip

ADA combines data from many source systems — not just CASB. So if you query the arrangements table without filtering, you'll get data from all sources.

Always join your arrangement queries with the AR Translation table to filter only CASB data. This is the most common mistake new analysts make.

SCD Tables — History Tracking
TypeMeaningUse Case
SCD0Static — no history keptReference data that never changes
SCD2Historical — full version historyTrack changes over time (e.g. balance history)

Example: The AI Measurement Spot table uses SCD2 to store a daily snapshot of account balances — so you can see what the balance was on any given date.

Placeholder Logic
🔄 What is a Placeholder?

Sometimes when CASB data arrives, reference data (like an Organisation Unit) isn't available yet. Instead of failing, ADA creates a placeholder record.

It generates a unique ID using MD5 hashing of: Institution code + Source system code + Account number.

When the correct data flows in from DRM later, it fills in the placeholder. No data loss!

Confidential Account Numbers
🔒 Masked by Default

When you query ADA, account numbers are masked by default. To see the full account number, you need confidential data access permissions.

To filter by account, use the AR Local ID field — it's available without confidential access.

Getting BSB / Branch Details

Need the BSB or branch info for an account? Follow this join path:

1
Arrangement (AR) Your starting point — the account record.
2
AR-IP-ORG Relationship Links the arrangement to its organisation unit.
3
IP_ORG + BSB Table Join here to get the branch name, BSB number, and location details.
Unity Catalog Structure
📂 New ADA Structure: ADA_SILVER_V

ADA now uses Unity Catalog. CASB product data is accessed by joining:
Prod Translate + Product Description (PD)
This gives you the human-readable product name, category, and full metadata.
